# Tighten a tourniquet's windlass to the green and lock it

A training thigh lies across the table with a windlass tourniquet strapped round it, snug. Beyond it, a pressure gauge
on the band's bladder reads 60 mmHg; its dial is green from 250. The instruction is: **"Tighten the tourniquet on the
training leg until its gauge reads green, then lock the rod in its clip."**

To do it, the robot has to:
- turn the windlass rod, which twists the band tighter and raises the gauge;
- keep hold of it: the band's pull turns the rod back the moment nothing holds or locks it;
- regrip as it goes: a gripper holding the rod turns it at most about 200 degrees before its wrist runs out, and the
  rod has to go 210, so something has to hold the rod while the hand takes a fresh grip (the other hand, as in the
  demo, or the clip itself);
- lock it only when the gauge is green. The clip takes the rod only when it lies along the thigh, which happens every
  half turn. The first time is 30 degrees in, with the gauge at about 90; locking there looks done but scores 0.25;
- lock it by pushing the rod's knee end down past the clip's lips (or lifting its hip end), then let go.

Bleeding is the leading cause of preventable death after injury, and for life-threatening bleeding from a limb a
windlass tourniquet is the standard first-aid tool. Courses such as Stop the Bleed teach the core of this task:
twist the windlass until the bleeding stops, then lock the rod in its clip (then secure it and note the time, which
this task leaves out). Here the gauge stands in for "until the bleeding stops". Robots that help with casualty care
need it too. The task tests three things:
- **Reading the goal from an instrument.** The instruction asks for the gauge in the green, not a number of turns,
  and the clip lines up before the gauge gets there.
- **Holding against a load.** The rod is always pulled back, so a regrip needs something else holding it first,
  and letting go before it is locked undoes the work.
- **Sequencing the regrip.** The demo turns with one hand and holds with the other on a rod only 15 cm long, so the
  hands have to keep clear of each other. One hand alone can also do it, by parking the rod in the clip while it
  regrips, or by pushing the rod round.

No task on the board tightens a tourniquet. The closest are #917 (release a phlebotomy tourniquet before withdrawing
the needle), #918 (add a fresh pad on top of a soaked dressing and hold it) and #409 (two-rescuer CPR on a manikin).

## Files

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `task.py` | The yam_arena task file: the tourniquet, the clip's hook, the band's pull, the gauge, the lock, the monitor, `success()` and `score()` |
| `geometry.py` | Dimensions and layout: the thigh, the band and windlass, the clip, the gauge, the pressure rule, the scoring tolerances |
| `build_assets.py` | Writes `assets/tourniquet.usda` and the dial's texture (needs `pxr`, PIL and the DejaVu fonts) |
| `assets/` | The tourniquet on its training thigh, with the gauge |
| `scenekit.py` | USD bodies and reset hooks for yam_arena task files |
| `demokit.py` | Helpers for the scripted demo |
| `demo.py` | The scripted full-score demo, and variants that lock early, keep holding, let go to regrip, or keep a hand on after locking |

`scenekit.py` hooks `yam_arena.scene` and the embodiment's step and reset without modifying yam_arena, so the task
runs with the standard `yam_arena` embodiment and `task_score` scorer. Tested with yam-isaac-lab-arena at `bd62771`
(Isaac Lab-Arena 0.3.1).

All the assets are generated by `build_assets.py` from `geometry.py`; there are no third-party models. The dial is
drawn with the DejaVu fonts (Bitstream Vera licence).

## Run a trial

From this folder, in a shell with the yam_arena environment active. New shells on the walkthrough's Brev instance
have it; elsewhere, `source <ARENA_DIR>/yam.env`.

```bash
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy dream \
  -E task=task.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 \
  --instruction "Tighten the tourniquet on the training leg until its gauge reads green, then lock the rod in its clip." \
  --scorer task_score --max-steps 2400 --store-frames --log-dir ~/yam-eval/tourniquet-dream
```

`task_score` prints the trial's score: 1.0 on success, otherwise `score()` at the end of the episode.

- **Seeds:** there is one fixed layout and no randomisation (`object_jitter=0`), so the seed has no effect.
- **Runtime:** a full 2,400-step Dream-1 trial took 6 min 31 s on an RTX 5090 while another simulation shared the GPU
  (mean round trip per inference 0.63 s), plus about a minute to start Isaac Sim. A successful trial ends earlier:
  the scripted demo stops after 553 steps.

Dream-1 scored 0. The right arm reached over the thigh toward the band and the gauge several times. At about 64 s it
pushed the rod round 9 degrees, raising the gauge to 69 mmHg, and the band turned it back; it never locked it. The
left arm stayed near its base. The run used one continuous Dream-1 session, with no errors or retries.

## Scripted demo

```bash
python demo.py task.py demo_out                     # full score: demo_*_cam.mp4, setup.png, result.json
python demo.py task.py early_out --early-lock       # locks the first time the rod lies along the thigh: 0.25
python demo.py task.py hold_out --hold              # turns it into the green but keeps holding it: 0.5
python demo.py task.py slip_out --slip              # lets go to regrip with nothing holding the rod: 0
python demo.py task.py keep_out --keep-hold         # locks it in the green but keeps a hand on it: 0.5
```

1. The right hand takes the rod's end from above, its grip leaning 15 degrees toward the robot, and turns it
   anticlockwise (seen from above). It passes the first time the rod lies along the thigh (30 degrees in, gauge
   about 90) and goes on to 100 degrees, where its wrist is near the end of its travel.
2. The left hand, leaning 30 degrees so the arms keep clear, takes the rod's other end and holds it still. The right
   lets go and takes the same end again with its wrist turned the other way round; the left lets go.
3. The right turns the rod on to 210 degrees, where it lies along the thigh again with the gauge in the green. It
   now holds the rod's hip end; it lifts it, watching the rod's tilt, until the knee end is down in the clip's hook,
   then lets go and backs off.

The hand's angle runs up to 10 degrees ahead of the rod's, because the wrist gives under the band's pull. Every move
follows the rod's measured angle and tilt, and the script checks the gauge on its needle before locking.

The demo succeeds (1.0) in 553 steps (28 s): the first grip turned the rod 100 degrees (the gauge at 160), the second
took it to 213 (273), and the rod locked with its knee end 7.7 mm down at the clip, its tip on the plate. Locking the first time the rod lies
along the thigh (`--early-lock`, the left hand lifting the hip end there) scores 0.25, locked at 92 mmHg. Keeping hold
in the green without locking (`--hold`) scores 0.5. Letting go to regrip with nothing holding the rod (`--slip`)
scores 0: the band turns it back to where it started. Locking in the green but keeping a hand on it (`--keep-hold`)
scores 0.5.

## Scoring

Judged at the end of the trial from a photo of the gauge and the clip, and the trial's video for letting go.

| Score | When |
|---|---|
| 1.0 (success) | The gauge reads 250 mmHg or more (the green), the rod is locked in the clip, and no part of the robot has come within 1 cm of the rod or the clip for the last 1 s. This ends the trial |
| 0.5 | The gauge is in the green but the rod is not locked and a hand still holds it, or it is locked with part of the robot within 1 cm of the rod or the clip in the last 1 s |
| 0.25 | The rod is locked in the hook but the gauge reads under 250 (locked too early) |
| 0 | Anything else: not tightened, or let go unlocked so the band turned it back |

- **Locked:** the rod lies along the thigh with its knee end pushed down past the clip's lips onto its plate, where
  the lips hold it against the band's pull. A rod resting between the prongs above the lips is not locked.
- **Chance:** the clip lines up every half turn. Locking at the first chance (the gauge at about 90) scores 0.25.
- **In simulation:**
  - **The gauge** reads 60 mmHg plus 1 mmHg per degree the rod has been turned, either way, from where it started;
    the needle shows it, and a stop holds it at 300.
  - **Locked:** the rod lies within 5 degrees of along the thigh and its centre line is 7 mm down at the hook, where
    its tip rests on the clip's plate. The lips are not modelled: from then on task code holds the rod down, as they would, until it
    is lifted back above 3 mm.
  - **Let go:** for the last 20 control steps, both arms stay at least 1 cm from the rod and the hook. The check uses
    yam_arena's collision hulls of every link, the gripper body and both fingers, at their simulated poses.

## Real-world build

All positions are in cm from the midpoint between the arm bases: x forward, y to the robot's left. Heights are above
the table top.

- **Training thigh.** A firm, skin-toned foam cylinder, 12.7 cm across and 40 cm long, lying across the table along
  y with its axis at x 28, 6.55 cm above the table; the hip end is on the robot's left. It rests on two pairs of
  wooden chocks (3 x 4 x 3.5 cm tall) at y +-15, their inner faces 5.6 cm either side of the axis, and is strapped
  down so it can't roll.
- **Tourniquet.** A blue training windlass tourniquet (CAT style), its band 3.8 cm wide, routed round the thigh at
  y 0 and fastened snug with its tail toward the arms. Its own windlass clip is removed (or taped flat) and replaced
  by the clip below. Under the band, the bladder of an adult blood pressure cuff (about 12 x 22 cm), wrapped once
  round the thigh, is tubed to the gauge with 5 mm rubber tubing.
  - Windlass rod: black plastic, 15 cm long, 12 mm across, 30 g, on the band's twisted strap; its axis is 14.7 cm
    above the table. It starts on top of the thigh 30 degrees off lying along it, one end toward the arms and the knee.
  - Clip: grey 3D-printed PLA, a plate (3.4 x 2.4 x 0.3 cm) glued on top of the thigh 6.4 cm toward the knee from
    the windlass, with two prongs 4 mm thick, 8 mm long and 7.5 mm tall, 4 mm clear either side of the rod. Their
    tops are 1.5 mm below the rod's underside, so the rod sweeps over them until its end is pushed down between
    them. Each prong has a 1 mm lip at its top, facing in, which the rod snaps past and which holds it down.
- **Gauge.** An aneroid gauge, 12 cm across, reading 0 to 300 mmHg, with a green arc from 250 to 300 and a stop at
  300, on a 14 mm square post on a 7 x 7 cm foot at x 43, y -15, its dial's centre 12 cm above the table, tilted 50
  degrees up toward the arms.
- **Calibration.** With the rod as set, the gauge reads 60. The simulation adds 1 mmHg per degree of turn, either
  way. On the real trainer, turn the rod by hand and check the gauge where the rod lies along the thigh:
  - turning anticlockwise (seen from above), about 90 (under 150) at 30 degrees and about 270 (at least 250) at 210;
  - turning clockwise, under 250 at 150 degrees.
  Start the band tighter or looser if not. Also check that the rod still sweeps over the prongs at 30 degrees and
  drops between them at 210: the strap bunches under it, and the clearance is only 1.5 mm.
- **Arms** start at the ready pose with open grippers.
- **Safety.** A training thigh only, never a person. The rod snaps back when released unlocked; keep hands clear of
  it and of the arms.

## Simulation fidelity

- Rigid bodies, 240 Hz physics, 20 Hz control.
- **The tourniquet** is an articulation fixed to the world: the thigh, chocks, band, clip plate and gauge are its base.
  - The windlass turns about the vertical on top of the band, and the rod can tip on it (a spring of 1.5 N m/rad, the
    strap holding it level, up to 10 degrees).
  - The band's pull is a torque from task code, 0.06 N m per radian of turn, back toward where it started.
  - The clip's prongs are fixed boxes that the rod's end sits between once locked; their lips are not modelled.
    Task code holds a locked rod down instead, as the lips would, until it is lifted back out.
- **Monitor:** after each control step, task code:
  - adds up the rod's turn (the joint reads modulo a turn) and sets the gauge's needle from it;
  - latches the lock when the rod lies along the thigh with its knee end 7 mm down at the hook, then holds it there
    (by moving the strap spring's target down) until it is lifted back above 3 mm;
  - checks how close the collision hulls of either arm, fingers included, come to the rod and the hook.
- **Simplified:** the real pressure under a band rises faster than linearly, and the real strap twists and bunches
  under the rod; here pressure is linear in the turn and the strap is not modelled. The real tourniquet's own clip
  is replaced by the glued clip.
- **Resets:** the reset untwists the rod, levels it, and zeroes the gauge.

## Reset between trials

Lift the rod out of the clip, untwist it back to its start (30 degrees off lying along the thigh, one end toward the
arms and the knee), and check the gauge reads 60 mmHg.
